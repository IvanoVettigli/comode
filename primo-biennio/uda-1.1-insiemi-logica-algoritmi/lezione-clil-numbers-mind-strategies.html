English Term & Audio Italian Equivalent Math Theory & Operational Context
1. Whole Numbers / Integers
Numeri Interi ($\mathbb{Z}$) Directed numbers without fractional parts: positive, negative, and zero ($\{\dots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \dots\}$); subtracting a negative moves right ($a - (-b) = a + b$).
2. Rational Numbers
Numeri Razionali ($\mathbb{Q}$) Any number expressible as a ratio $\frac{a}{b}$ of two integers ($b \neq 0$); $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense on the number line (midpoint $\frac{a+b}{2}$).
3. Numerator & Denominator
Numeratore e Denominatore In $\frac{3}{4}$, $3$ is the numerator (how many parts) and $4$ is the denominator (the slice size / unit partition).
4. Common Denominator
Denominatore Comune (m.c.m.) A shared multiple (Least Common Multiple, LCM) needed to add or subtract fractions of equal size: $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$.
5. Reciprocal
Inverso / Reciproco The multiplicative inverse: the reciprocal of $\frac{3}{5}$ is $\frac{5}{3}$ because $\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{5}{3} = 1$. Also linked to negative exponents: $(\frac{a}{b})^{-1} = \frac{b}{a}$.
6. Cross-Simplification
Semplificazione in croce Cancelling common factors between numerators and denominators before multiplying: $\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{4 \times 5} = \frac{3}{20}$.
7. Distributive Property
Proprietà Distributiva Multiplying a sum or difference term by term: $a \times (b \pm c) = ab \pm ac$ (e.g. $19 \times 35 = (20-1)\times 35$).
8. Mental Computation
Calcolo Mentale Guidato Calculating accurately in your head by choosing comfortable representations ($36 \times 0.25 = 36 \times \frac{1}{4} = 9$).
9. Scientific Notation
Notazione Scientifica & OdG Writing numbers as $a \times 10^n$ with $1 \le a < 10$ and $n \in \mathbb{Z}$; the nearest power of ten is its order of magnitude.
10. Prime Factorization
Numeri Primi e Scomposizione Every integer $>1$ is either prime or a unique product of primes (composite). Euclid proved there are infinitely many primes via $N = (p_1 \cdots p_k) + 1$.
11. Proof by Contradiction
Dimostrazione per Assurdo Assuming the negation of the conclusion ($\neg q$) alongside the hypothesis ($p$) to derive an impossible contradiction. One single counterexample disproves a "for all" claim!
12. Flowchart & Trace Table
Diagramma di Flusso e Traccia An algorithm is a finite, unambiguous sequence of steps. A trace table tracks variable values through if-then-else branches and loops.

p ⇒ q (A ∩ B)
"if p, then q" (or "p implies q") • $A \cap B$ = "A intersection B".
⚠️ Watch Out! International Notation Traps & Math False Friends (IT vs EN):
  • Punto e Virgola invertiti nei numeri! In inglese il punto separa i decimali (0.25 = $0,25$), mentre la virgola separa le migliaia (2,000,000 = due milioni!). Non scambiare mai l'inglese 1,500 ($1\,500$) per $1,5$!
  • Even vs Odd: even number = numero pari ($2, 4, 6\dots$); odd number = numero dispari ($1, 3, 5\dots$).
  • False Friends nei teoremi: "Assume that..." significa «Supponiamo per ipotesi che...» (non «assumere un lavoratore»); "A logical argument" significa «un ragionamento / argomentazione logica» (non «un litigio» né «un tema scolastico», che si dice topic).

Task 1 • Fractions in Sociology +14 XP

The High School Social Survey

🔗
Theory ↔ Practice Connection: Applies Theory 2 • Rational Numbers and Theory 6 • Cross-Simplification (computing a fraction of a fraction of a sample).
"A group of sociology students surveyed 400 high school teenagers about their leisure reading habits. They found that $\frac{3}{8}$ of the students read non-fiction articles, and $\frac{2}{5}$ of those non-fiction readers are specifically interested in psychology essays. How many students in total read psychology essays?"
Un gruppo di studenti di sociologia ha intervistato $400$ liceali sulle abitudini di lettura. Hanno rilevato che i $\frac{3}{8}$ leggono articoli di saggistica e i $\frac{2}{5}$ di questi sono interessati a saggi di psicologia. Quanti studenti in totale leggono saggi di psicologia?
Cross-simplify $\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{2}{5}$ first: divide $2$ and $8$ by $2$ to get $\frac{3}{4 \times 5} = \frac{3}{20}$. Now compute $\frac{3}{20} \times 400$: since $400 : 20 = 20$, multiply $3 \times 20$!

Task 3 • Scientific Notation & English Punctuation +14 XP

UNESCO Youth Volunteering Report

🔗
Theory ↔ Practice Connection: Connects Theory 9 • Scientific Notation and the English Decimal Point vs Comma Rule.
"An international youth report states that out of 2,000,000 students across partner schools, a fraction equal to 0.15 (fifteen percent) took part in local volunteering projects. Express the number of youth volunteers in scientific notation $a \times 10^n$ (with $1 \le a < 10$)."
Un rapporto internazionale afferma che su $2\,000\,000$ di studenti (nota le virgole inglesi per le migliaia!), una quota pari a $0,15$ (nota il punto decimale inglese!) ha partecipato a progetti di volontariato. Esprimi il numero dei volontari in notazione scientifica $a \times 10^n$.
First: $2,000,000 = 2 \times 10^6$. Multiply by $0.15 = \frac{15}{100}$: $2,000,000 \times 0.15 = 300,000$ (three hundred thousand). Count the $5$ zeros after $3$: $300,000 = 3 \times 10^5$!

Task 4 • Primes & Logical Counterexamples +14 XP

Disproving a False Claim & Building Euclid's Number

🔗
Theory ↔ Practice Connection: Integrates Theory 10 • Prime Numbers & Euclid and Theory 11 • Counterexamples in Logic.
"Part A (Counterexample): A classmate claims: 'Every odd integer greater than 1 is a prime number.' Find the smallest odd counterexample that proves this statement false.
Part B (Euclid's Proof): Assume a finite list of primes $L = \{2, 3, 5\}$. Compute Euclid's number $N = (2 \times 3 \times 5) + 1$."
Parte A: Un compagno afferma: «Ogni numero intero dispari (*odd*) maggiore di $1$ è un numero primo». Trova il più piccolo controesempio dispari che dimostra che la frase è falsa.
Parte B: Supponiamo (*Assume*) una lista finita di primi $\{2, 3, 5\}$. Calcola il numero di Euclide $N = (2 \times 3 \times 5) + 1$.
• Odd means dispari: $3, 5, 7$ are prime, so check the next odd number ($9 = 3 \times 3$, composite!).
• For Euclid's number: $2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30$, plus $1$ gives $31$.
Task 5 • Algorithms & Trace Table +14 XP

Erasmus Exchange Selection Algorithm

🔗
Theory ↔ Practice Connection: Applies Theory 12 • Algorithm, Conditional if-then-else & Trace Table.
"Trace the following English pseudocode used to compute a student's Erasmus priority score:
1. score ← 12
2. if (volunteer = YES) then score ← score + 8 else score ← score + 3
3. if (english_grade ≥ 7) then score ← score × 2
4. output score
Compute the final score for Emma (volunteer = YES, english_grade = 8) and for Liam (volunteer = NO, english_grade = 6)."
Traccia lo pseudocodice inglese che calcola il punteggio score per lo scambio Erasmus. Si parte da score ← 12. Se lo studente fa volontariato somma $8$, altrimenti somma $3$. Poi, se il voto in inglese è $\ge 7$, raddoppia score. Calcola il punteggio finale di Emma (`YES`, voto `8`) e di Liam (`NO`, voto `6`).
• Emma: starts at $12 \to 12 + 8 = 20$ (`YES`) $\to$ since $8 \ge 7$ is True, $20 \times 2 = 40$.
• Liam: starts at $12 \to 12 + 3 = 15$ (`NO`) $\to$ since $6 \ge 7$ is False, line 3 does not double it, so it stays $15$!

The Real-World Scenario (La Situazione-Problema):
Your Human Sciences class is organizing an international Solidarity Bake Sale with an Erasmus partner school to fund books for a community youth center. A traditional oat-cake recipe for 1 baking tray requires:
  • Flour (Farina): $\frac{3}{8}\text{ kg}$ per tray
  • Brown Sugar (Zucchero di canna): $\frac{1}{4}\text{ kg}$ per tray
  • Oat Flakes (Fiocchi d'avena): $\frac{1}{6}\text{ kg}$ per tray
Your team decides to bake 24 trays in total, selling each tray for €19, with ingredient expenses equal to €76.
  1. Common Denominator (Taglia Comune): Compute the total dry weight of 1 single tray ($\frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{6}\text{ kg}$) by finding the lowest common denominator ($24$) and writing the steps in English.
