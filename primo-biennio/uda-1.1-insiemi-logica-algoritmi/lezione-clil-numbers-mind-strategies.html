1. Whole Numbers / Integers
Numeri Interi ($\mathbb{Z}$) Directed numbers without fractional parts: positive, negative and zero ($\{\dots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \dots\}$); subtracting a negative moves right ($a - (-b) = a + b$).
2. Rational Numbers
Numeri Razionali ($\mathbb{Q}$) Any number expressible as the quotient $\frac{a}{b}$ of two integers ($b \neq 0$), or as a terminating/repeating decimal ($0.25 = \frac{1}{4}$).
3. Numerator & Denominator
Numeratore e Denominatore In $\frac{3}{4}$, $3$ is the numerator (how many slices) and $4$ is the denominator (the slice size / unit partition).
4. Common Denominator
Denominatore Comune A shared multiple of the denominators needed to add or subtract fractions of equal size: $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$.
5. Reciprocal
Inverso / Reciproco The multiplicative inverse: the reciprocal of $\frac{3}{5}$ is $\frac{5}{3}$ because $\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{5}{3} = 1$. Dividing by a fraction equals multiplying by its reciprocal.
6. Cross-Simplification
Semplificazione in croce Cancelling common factors between numerators and denominators before multiplying: $\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{4 \times 5} = \frac{3}{20}$.
7. Distributive Property
Proprietà Distributiva Multiplying a sum or difference by multiplying each term separately: $a \times (b \pm c) = ab \pm ac$.
8. Mental Computation
Calcolo Mentale Guidato Calculating accurately in your mind by choosing comfortable representations ($36 \times 0.25 = 36 \times \frac{1}{4} = 9$).

Task 1 • Human Sciences Context +15 XP

The High School Social Survey

🔗
Theory ↔ Practice Connection (Collegamento Teoria ↔ Pratica): This task deepens Theory 2 • Rational Numbers ($\mathbb{Q}$) and Theory 6 • Cross-Simplification (finding a fraction of a fraction of a population by cancelling common factors before multiplying).
"A group of sociology students surveyed 400 high school teenagers about their leisure reading habits. They found that $\frac{3}{8}$ of the students read non-fiction articles, and $\frac{2}{5}$ of those non-fiction readers are specifically interested in psychology essays. How many students in total read psychology essays?"
Un gruppo di studenti di sociologia ha intervistato 400 liceali. Hanno rilevato che i $\frac{3}{8}$ leggono saggistica e i $\frac{2}{5}$ di questi sono specificamente interessati a saggi di psicologia. Quanti studenti in totale leggono saggi di psicologia?

💡 Mental Shortcut (Cross-Simplification):
We need to calculate $\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{2}{5} \times 400$. Notice that $\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3 \times 1}{4 \times 5} = \frac{3}{20}$.
Now calculate $\frac{3}{20} \times 400$: since $400 : 20 = 20$, then $3 \times 20 = \dots$

Task 2 • Distributive Strategy +15 XP

Library Book Order with Smart Multiplication

🔗
Theory ↔ Practice Connection (Collegamento Teoria ↔ Pratica): This task deepens Theory 7 • Distributive Property and Theory 8 • Mental Computation (rewriting a factor near a round tens boundary as $19 = 20 - 1$ to compute $(a - b) \times c = ac - bc$ effortlessly).
"The school library is purchasing 19 copies of a Developmental Psychology handbook. Each copy costs €35. Instead of doing long vertical multiplication on paper, the school secretary uses the distributive property: $$19 \times 35 = (20 - 1) \times 35 = (20 \times 35) - (1 \times 35)$$ What is the total cost in Euros?"
La biblioteca scolastica acquista 19 copie di un manuale di psicologia dello sviluppo a 35€ ciascuna. Usando $(20 - 1) \times 35$, calcola mentalmente la spesa totale.

💡 Mental Computation:
$20 \times 35 = 700$. Now subtract $1 \times 35 = 35$: calculate $700 - 35 = \dots$

The Real-World Scenario (La Situazione-Problema):
Your Human Sciences class is organizing an international Solidarity Bake Sale to fund books for a partner school library. A traditional oat-cake recipe for 1 baking tray requires:
  • Flour (Farina): $\frac{3}{8}\text{ kg}$ per tray
  • Brown Sugar (Zucchero di canna): $\frac{1}{4}\text{ kg}$ per tray
  • Oat Flakes (Fiocchi d'avena): $\frac{1}{6}\text{ kg}$ per tray
Your team decides to bake 24 trays in total, selling each tray for €19.
  1. Common Denominator (Taglia Comune): Compute the total dry weight of 1 single tray ($\frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{6}\text{ kg}$) by finding the lowest common denominator and writing the step in English.
  2. Cross-Simplification (Semplificazione in Croce): Calculate how many kilograms of flour, brown sugar, and oat flakes are needed for 24 trays by cross-simplifying each fraction with $24$ before multiplying.
  3. Distributive Mental Strategy (Calcolo Mentale Distributivo): If all $24$ trays are sold at €19 each, compute the gross revenue $24 \times 19$ mentally using $24 \times (20 - 1)$, then subtract €76 of ingredient expenses to find the net charity fund.
  4. Bilingual Reflection (Scienze Umane & CLIL): Write 4–5 sentences in English (using at least 3 terms from the Glossary) explaining how clear quantitative planning supports teamwork and social solidarity projects.
