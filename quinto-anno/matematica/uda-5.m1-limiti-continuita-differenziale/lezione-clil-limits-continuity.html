CLIL Task 1 • Spoken Calculus

How to Read Calculus Aloud: Spoken Formulas in English

All'Esame di Stato e nei contesti internazionali, leggere correttamente le formule matematiche in inglese dimostra padronanza scientifica. Clicca su 🔊 Listen per ascoltare la voce nativa, ripeti ad alta voce e usa 🇮🇹 Traduzione per chiarire ogni passaggio.

Finite Limit
$$\lim_{x \to 3} f(x) = 5$$

"The limit of f of x as x approaches 3 is equal to 5."

Italiano: «Il limite di f di x per x che tende a 3 è uguale a 5».
One-Sided Limits
$$\lim_{x \to 2^+} f(x) = +\infty$$

"The limit of f of x as x approaches 2 from the right is positive infinity."

Italiano: «Il limite di f di x per x che tende a 2 da destra è più infinito» (limite destro = right-hand limit).
Indeterminate Form
$$\left[\frac{0}{0}\right], \quad \left[\frac{\infty}{\infty}\right]$$

"Zero over zero indeterminate form; infinity over infinity indeterminate form."

Italiano: «Forma indeterminata zero su zero; infinito su infinito».
Continuity Definition
$$\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = f(x_0)$$

"The limit of f of x as x approaches x naught equals f of x naught."

Italiano: «Il limite di f(x) per x che tende a x con zero è uguale al valore f(x con zero)». Nota: $x_0$ si pronuncia "x naught" (UK) o "x zero / x sub zero" (US).
Derivative & Difference Quotient
$$f'(x_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h}$$

"f prime of x naught equals the limit as h approaches zero of the difference quotient."

Italiano: «f primo di x con zero è uguale al limite per h che tende a zero del rapporto incrementale (difference quotient)».
Tangent Line Equation
$$y - f(x_0) = f'(x_0)(x - x_0)$$

"y minus f of x naught equals f prime of x naught times the quantity x minus x naught."

Italiano: «Equazione della retta tangente: y meno f(x con zero) uguale a f primo per (x meno x con zero)».
CLIL Task 2 • Technical Lexicon

The Calculus Lexicon: Glossary & False Friends in Social Sciences

I termini matematici inglesi possono trarre in inganno. Esplora il glossario sonoro ed esamina i Falsi Amici per evitare scivoloni linguistici.

English Term Audio & IPA Meaning in Calculus Italian Equivalent
Rate of change The ratio measuring how quickly a dependent variable changes with respect to an independent variable. Velocità / Tasso di variazione
Approach To get arbitrarily close to a target point without necessarily reaching it ($x \to x_0$). Tendere / Avvicinarsi
Difference quotient The fraction $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)}{h}$ representing average slope. Rapporto incrementale
Secant line A line that intersects a curve at two or more distinct points. Retta secante
Tangent line The limiting position of secant lines, kissing the curve at point $P$ with slope $f'(x_0)$. Retta tangente
Smooth function A differentiable function whose graph exhibits no sharp corners, cusps, or breaks. Funzione liscia / derivabile
Marginal cost The cost of producing one additional unit of a good: $C'(q) = \frac{dC}{dq}$. Costo marginale
Break-even point The production volume where profit equals zero, guaranteed by the Intermediate Value Theorem. Punto di pareggio / radice $f(x)=0$

⚠️ False Friends & Language Traps to Avoid

  • Continuous vs Continual: In English, continuous means uninterrupted in space or time (unbroken curve: $f$ has no gaps). Continual means recurring repeatedly with interruptions (e.g., continual phone ringing). Never say *"continual function"*!
  • Derivative: In calculus, it is the rate of change $f'(x)$. In financial economics, a derivative (strumento derivato) is a tradable contract (options, futures) whose price is derived from an underlying asset!
  • Actual: Does NOT mean "attuale" (which translates to current or present). Actual means effettivo / reale (e.g. *"the actual value $f(x_0)$ may differ from the limit $L$"*).
  • Rate: Can mean velocità (rate of change), aliquota (tax rate), tasso d'interesse (interest rate), or the verb valutare (to rate an experience).
CLIL Task 3 • Geometry & Singularities

Classifying Non-Differentiable Features in English

Read the following three mathematical definitions in English. Test your comprehension by matching each definition with its correct geometric feature and listening to the pronunciation.

Feature A
Corner Point

"The left-hand and right-hand derivatives are both finite numbers, but they do not match: $f'_-(x_0) \neq f'_+(x_0)$."

Key Example: $f(x) = |x|$ at $x = 0$, where $f'_-(0) = -1$ and $f'_+(0) = +1$.
Feature B
Cusp

"Both one-sided derivatives explode to infinity with opposite signs ($f'_-(x_0) = -\infty$ and $f'_+(x_0) = +\infty$), creating a sharp point."

Key Example: $f(x) = \sqrt{|x|}$ at $x = 0$, where the tangent is vertical.
Feature C
Vertical Tangent Inflection

"Both one-sided derivatives approach infinity with the same sign ($f'_-(x_0) = f'_+(x_0) = +\infty$). The curve crosses a vertical tangent."

Key Example: $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}$ at $x = 0$.
CLIL Task 4 • Applied Sociology Case Study

The Rogers Diffusion Curve: Rate of Cultural & Technological Adoption

In sociology and cultural studies, sociologist Everett Rogers (1962) modeled the Diffusion of Innovations. The cumulative proportion of individuals adopting a new social practice or technology over time $t$ (in months) follows a logistic sigmoid curve:

$$P(t) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-0.4(t - 10)}}$$
Sociological Question:
What does the derivative $P'(t) = \frac{dP}{dt}$ represent, and when does the cultural adoption rate reach its maximum?

1. The Derivative as Adoption Speed:
While $P(t)$ measures the cumulative percentage of adopters (from 0 to 1, or 0% to 100%), the derivative $P'(t)$ measures the instantaneous speed of adoption (new adopters per month).

2. The Inflection Point ($t = 10$ months):
At $t = 10$, $P(10) = \frac{1}{1 + 1} = 0.50$ (50% of the population has adopted the innovation). At this exact inflection point, the tangent line reaches its maximum steepness ($P'(10) = 0.10$ or 10% per month). After $t = 10$, the market becomes saturated, the derivative $P'(t)$ decreases, and the curve flattens toward the horizontal asymptote $y = 1$ ($100\%$).

CLIL Task 5 • Global Policy & Economics

Welfare Taper Rates: Why Public Policy Requires Mathematical Continuity

In comparative welfare state research, economists analyze how government benefits decrease as household earned income $x$ rises. Read the following excerpt from an international policy report:

"A fundamental principle in optimal taxation is the avoidance of cliff-edge discontinuities. When a social assistance program abruptly terminates benefits at an income threshold $x_0$ (creating a jump discontinuity of first species), the effective marginal tax rate exceeds 100%. Consequently, a citizen earning an additional pound or euro loses hundreds of pounds in net benefits, giving rise to the so-called poverty trap. Modern social protection schemes therefore employ continuous tapering functions, ensuring that the net disposable income function $N(x) = x + B(x) - T(x)$ is continuous everywhere."
Traduzione in Italiano: «Un principio fondamentale nella tassazione ottimale è l'eliminazione delle discontinuità a gradino (cliff-edges). Quando un sussidio cessa bruscamente al superamento di una soglia di reddito (creando una discontinuità di 1ª specie con salto), l'aliquota marginale effettiva supera il 100%: guadagnare un euro in più fa perdere centinaia di euro di sussidio, intrappolando il cittadino nella povertà. I sistemi moderni adottano perciò funzioni di raccordo continuo (tapering), garantendo la continuità matematica del reddito netto disponibile».
