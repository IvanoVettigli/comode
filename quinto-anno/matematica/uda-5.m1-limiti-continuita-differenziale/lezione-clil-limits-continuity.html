CLIL Task 1 • Spoken Calculus +14 XP

How to Read Calculus Aloud: Spoken Formulas in English

All'Esame di Stato e nei contesti accademici internazionali, saper leggere ad alta voce le formule matematiche in inglese dimostra piena padronanza interdisciplinare. Clicca su 🔊 Listen per ascoltare la pronuncia madrelingua, ripeti ad alta voce e usa 🇮🇹 Traduzione per verificare ogni espressione.

1. Finite Limit
$$\lim_{x \to 3} f(x) = 5$$

"The limit of f of x as x approaches 3 is equal to 5."

Italiano: «Il limite di $f(x)$ per $x$ che tende a $3$ è uguale a $5$». Nota: il verbo tendere a si traduce con approaches (o tends to).
2. One-Sided Limits
$$\lim_{x \to 2^+} f(x) = +\infty$$

"The limit of f of x as x approaches 2 from the right is positive infinity."

Italiano: «Il limite di $f(x)$ per $x$ che tende a $2$ da destra è più infinito» (limite destro = right-hand limit; da sinistra = from the left).
3. Indeterminate Form
$$\left[\frac{0}{0}\right], \quad \left[\frac{\infty}{\infty}\right]$$

"Zero over zero indeterminate form; infinity over infinity indeterminate form."

Italiano: «Forma indeterminata zero su zero; infinito su infinito». La linea di frazione si legge over.
4. Continuity Definition
$$\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = f(x_0)$$

"The limit of f of x as x approaches x naught equals f of x naught."

Italiano: «Il limite di $f(x)$ per $x$ che tende a $x$ con zero è uguale a $f(x_0)$». Nota: $x_0$ si pronuncia "x naught" (UK/US) oppure "x sub zero".
5. Derivative & Difference Quotient
$$f'(x_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h}$$

"f prime of x naught equals the limit as h approaches zero of the difference quotient."

Italiano: «$f$ primo di $x_0$ è uguale al limite per $h$ che tende a zero del rapporto incrementale (difference quotient)». L'apice della derivata $f'$ si legge f prime!
6. Tangent Line Equation
$$y - f(x_0) = f'(x_0)(x - x_0)$$

"y minus f of x naught equals f prime of x naught times the quantity x minus x naught."

Italiano: «Equazione della retta tangente: $y - f(x_0)$ uguale a $f'(x_0)$ che moltiplica aperta tonda $(x - x_0)$ (times the quantity $x$ minus $x$ naught)».

CLIL Task 2 • Technical Lexicon +14 XP

The Calculus Lexicon: Glossary & False Friends in Social Sciences

I termini matematici inglesi possono nascondere insidie lessicali e di punteggiatura. Esplora il glossario sonoro con trascrizione fonetica IPA ed esamina i False Friends da evitare al colloquio.

English Term Audio & IPA Meaning in Calculus Italian Equivalent
Rate of change The ratio measuring how quickly a dependent variable changes with respect to an independent variable. Velocità / Tasso di variazione
Approach To get arbitrarily close to a target point without necessarily reaching it ($x \to x_0$). Tendere / Avvicinarsi
Difference quotient The fraction $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)}{h}$ representing average slope. Rapporto incrementale
Secant line A line that intersects a curve at two distinct points $P$ and $Q$. Retta secante
Tangent line The limiting position of secant lines, touching the curve at point $P$ with slope $f'(x_0)$. Retta tangente
Smooth function A differentiable function whose graph exhibits no sharp corners, cusps, or breaks. Funzione liscia / derivabile
Marginal cost The cost of producing one additional unit of a good: $C'(q) = \frac{dC}{dq}$. Costo marginale
Break-even point The production volume where net profit equals zero, guaranteed by the Intermediate Value Theorem. Punto di pareggio ($P(q)=0$)

⚠️ False Friends & International Notation Traps to Avoid

  • Continuous vs Continual: In English, continuous means uninterrupted in space or time (an unbroken curve: $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = f(x_0)$). Continual means recurring repeatedly with pauses (e.g., continual interruptions). Never say "continual function"!
  • Actual vs Current: Actual does NOT mean "attuale" (which translates to current or present). Actual means effettivo / reale (e.g., "the actual value $f(x_0)$ coincides with the limit $L$").
  • Derivative (Calculus vs Finance): In mathematics, it is the instantaneous rate of change $f'(x)$. In economics and finance, a financial derivative (strumento derivato) is a contract whose value derives from an underlying asset.
  • Decimal Point vs Thousand Comma: English uses a point for decimals (0.25 = $0,25$) and a comma for thousands (2,000 = duemila!).

1. Corner Point (Punto Angoloso)

"The left-hand and right-hand derivatives are both finite numbers, but they do not match: $f'_-(x_0) \neq f'_+(x_0)$."

Key Example: $f(x) = |x|$ at $x = 0$, where $f'_-(0) = -1$ and $f'_+(0) = +1$.
Feature B • Click to View
2. Cusp (Cuspide)

"Both one-sided derivatives explode to infinity with opposite signs ($f'_-(x_0) = -\infty$ and $f'_+(x_0) = +\infty$), creating a sharp peak."

Key Example: $f(x) = \sqrt{|x|}$ at $x = 0$, where the tangent is vertical.
Feature C • Click to View
3. Vertical Tangent Inflection

"Both one-sided derivatives approach infinity with the same sign ($f'_-(x_0) = f'_+(x_0) = +\infty$). The curve crosses a vertical tangent."

Key Example: $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}$ at $x = 0$.

The Rogers Diffusion Curve: Rate of Cultural & Technological Adoption

In sociology and communication studies, Everett Rogers (1962) modeled the Diffusion of Innovations. The cumulative proportion of citizens adopting a new social practice or technology over time $t$ (in months) follows a logistic sigmoid function:

$$P(t) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-0.4(t - 10)}}$$

1. The Derivative as Adoption Speed: While $P(t)$ measures the cumulative percentage of adopters (from $0\%$ to $100\%$), the derivative $P'(t)$ measures the instantaneous speed of adoption (new adopters per month).

2. The Inflection Point ($t = 10$ months): At $t = 10$, $P(10) = 0.50$ ($50\%$ of the population). At this exact inflection point, the tangent line reaches its maximum steepness ($P'(10) = 0.10 = 10\%/\text{month}$). Afterwards, the market saturates and $P(t)$ approaches the horizontal asymptote $y = 1$ ($100\%$).

CLIL Task 5 • Global Policy & Economics +14 XP

Welfare Taper Rates: Why Public Policy Requires Mathematical Continuity

In comparative welfare state research, economists analyze how government benefits decrease as household earned income $x$ rises. Read the following excerpt from an international policy report:

"A fundamental principle in optimal taxation is the avoidance of cliff-edge discontinuities. When a social assistance program abruptly terminates benefits at an income threshold $x_0$ (creating a jump discontinuity of first species), the effective marginal tax rate exceeds $100\%$. Consequently, a citizen earning an additional pound or euro loses hundreds of pounds in net benefits, giving rise to the so-called poverty trap. Modern social protection schemes therefore employ continuous tapering functions, ensuring that the net disposable income function $N(x) = x + B(x) - T(x)$ is continuous everywhere."
Traduzione in Italiano: «Un principio fondamentale nella tassazione ottimale è l'eliminazione delle discontinuità a gradino (cliff-edges). Quando un sussidio cessa bruscamente al superamento di una soglia di reddito $x_0$ (creando una discontinuità di 1ª specie con salto), l'aliquota marginale effettiva supera il $100\%$: guadagnare un euro in più fa perdere centinaia di euro di sussidio, intrappolando il cittadino nella povertà (poverty trap). I sistemi moderni adottano perciò funzioni di raccordo continuo (tapering), garantendo la continuità matematica del reddito netto disponibile».
